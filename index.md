I am a PhD Fellow at the Department of Strategy and Innovation, Copenhagen Business School, where I study the economics of science and innovation with a focus on gender and academic knowledge work. My research asks how geographic mobility, institutional structures, and funding processes shape who participates in and benefits from the production and diffusion of scientific knowledge, and why women remain underrepresented in high-visibility roles such as expert policy advising.

My job market paper shows that women academic economists are less likely than men to testify as expert witnesses before the U.S. Congress, and that geographic mobility friction is an important, previously overlooked part of the explanation. I work empirically at scale, combining large administrative datasets with topic modeling and machine learning.

I hold an MA (with distinction) in International Business Communication from Copenhagen Business School and have been a visiting PhD fellow at the House of Innovation, Stockholm School of Economics, and at Bocconi University's Department of Management and Technology. My work is supported by the Carlsberg Foundation Young Researcher Fellowship and the Danish Research Council's Sapere Aude grant.
