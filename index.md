I am a PhD Fellow at the Department of Strategy and Innovation, Copenhagen Business School. My research sits at the intersection of the economics of science and innovation, organization design, and gender economics, with a particular focus on the drivers of gender inequality in representation and its consequences for individuals and organizations. My research asks how organizational design, institutional structures, and geographic mobility shape who participates in and benefits from the production, diffusion, and application of scientific knowledge.

My job market paper (co-authored with Yotam Sofer) examines why women remain underrepresented in high-level expert advisory work despite having comparable scientific qualifications. Whereas prior research has largely attributed this gap to differences in preferences or gatekeepers' selection decisions, this paper asks whether geographic mobility frictions constitute an overlooked supply side constraint that limits women's participation in episodic advisory work. 

Constructing a dataset covering the universe of academic economists who testified before the U.S. Congress between 1969 and 2023, together with a large comparison sample of intellectually proximate scholars, we find that women academic economists have lower odds than comparable men of appearing as expert witnesses before the U.S. Congress, that geographic distance attenuates this gap, and that the exogenous shift to virtual hearings during COVID-19 reversed the gap. Together, this points to geographic mobility friction as an important driver of women's participation in expert advisory work, with WFA policies as an effective remidy. 

I hold an MA from Copenhagen Business School and have been a visiting PhD fellow at the House of Innovation, Stockholm School of Economics, and at Bocconi University's Department of Management and Technology.
